Privacy

Navisual sends only your active window — never your full desktop — and only to the AI provider you choose. Screenshots stay in memory and are never written to disk.

The free tier is free because its AI provider may retain and train on your requests; paid tiers, your own API key, and offline Ollama don't. Full details in our Privacy Policy.

Frequently asked

What does Navisual do, and who is it for?

It watches your active window and gives you real-time, step-by-step guidance for whatever task you describe — pointing at the exact button or menu to click next, with audio narration. It's for anyone stuck on a task in an unfamiliar app: learning new software, walking a family member through a setup wizard, or just not remembering where a setting lives. Think of it as someone experienced sitting next to you and pointing — except you're always the one clicking.

Does the AI control my mouse?

No. Navisual only points at where to click and narrates the next step. Every click, keystroke, and confirmation is yours.

Does it work in the browser, and with the command line?

Both. Web pages are among the surfaces Navisual reads most reliably — web apps, forms, admin panels and settings pages all work, and Chrome and Edge get a dedicated fast path. For the terminal, Navisual never runs anything: when a step involves a command, it puts the exact text on your clipboard so you can read it, then paste and press Enter yourself. Same for URLs, filenames, and anything else you'd otherwise retype. A task that spans a browser download, a text editor and a terminal is fine — Navisual follows whichever window you're working in.

What data leaves my machine?

A screenshot of your active window (not the full desktop) plus your conversation text, sent to whichever AI provider you choose. Element matching runs locally. Note: the free tier's AI models may retain and train on those requests (screenshot included); paid tiers don't under their providers' current policies, and Ollama keeps everything local — see the Privacy Policy.

Can it run fully offline?

Yes. Choose Ollama as your provider and pull a vision model (e.g. llama3.2-vision). Nothing leaves your machine.

Which Windows versions are supported?

Windows 10 (1809 or later) and Windows 11. WebView2 is required — it ships with Windows 11 and installs automatically on Windows 10.

Is it open source?

Source-available under FSL-1.1-Apache-2.0. Each version converts to Apache 2.0 two years after release.

Windows says “Windows protected your PC” — is it safe?

Yes. That’s Microsoft SmartScreen, which warns on any newly-released app until it has been downloaded enough to build reputation — it’s about download volume, not safety, and it appears even for properly code-signed apps. Navisual’s installer is code-signed (Azure Trusted Signing) and fully source-available (FSL). To continue, click More info, then Run anyway. The warning disappears as more people install.

What happens after the free 30 requests?

Top up the managed tier with pay-as-you-go coins (from $5), bring your own API key (Anthropic, Gemini, OpenAI, DeepSeek, Qwen), or run locally with Ollama for unlimited free use.

macOS or Linux?

Windows only for now. macOS and Linux ports are planned after the 1.0 public launch.
